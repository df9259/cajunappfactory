StopScope privacy policy

Effective October 6, 2026. This privacy policy covers StopScope, the route planner app from Cajun App Factory. The app was called MyStops before this rename. Questions about this policy go to support@cajunappfactory.com.

Information the app uses

With your permission, StopScope uses your location to show where you are, order your stops, and show miles and time left on the route. With your permission, it uses the camera or a photo you choose to read an address from a label or a list. A proof photo you take stays on your phone with that stop. Stop addresses are used to build the route. The app does not ask for your contacts, card number, or device identifiers such as an IMEI.

Location in the background (optional). If you turn on Background mileage tracking, StopScope collects location data to log the miles you drive on a route, even when the app is closed or not in use (for example while Google Maps is open). A notification shows while it runs, and you can stop it there or turn it off in Settings. It is used only to add up miles on your phone, is not sent to us or anyone else, and the app keeps daily mile totals, not a trail of places.

Microphone. If you tap the mic to say an address, StopScope uses Android’s speech recognition service, which may send the audio to its provider (usually Google) to turn it into text. StopScope gets only the text and does not record or store audio.

Kept on your phone. StopScope also stores what you enter or bring in: your home or start address, stop and package notes, delivery windows, spreadsheets you import, odometer readings, fuel amounts and costs, and odometer and receipt photos. Text in those photos is read on the phone. These stay on your phone, except that addresses are sent to Google to place them, as described below.

Who it is shared with

Stop addresses and, when the app places stops or draws the road line, your location are sent to Google (Maps SDK for Android, Geocoding API, and Routes API). We do not keep a copy of those requests. The next stop opens in Google Maps, which then handles the address under its own policy. Waze is coming soon. We do not sell this information, and we do not share it with advertisers. We may disclose information if the law requires it, or as part of a sale of the app, and we will say so if that happens.

If Google can’t name a spot you long-press on the map, its coordinates are sent to OpenStreetMap’s Nominatim service to look up an address. If the phone’s built-in text reader is not available, the app downloads a backup text reader from the jsDelivr network. Only that download request goes to jsDelivr; your photos are not sent.

How it is handled

Android asks for location and camera access when you first open StopScope. You can deny either and still type stops. Android asks for the microphone the first time you tap the mic. Background mileage tracking is explained in the app before Android asks for it. The connections to Google, OpenStreetMap, and jsDelivr are encrypted. We do not run our own server for your routes, photos, or mileage. There is no account with us.

How long it is kept

We do not keep your routes, photos, or mileage records. StopScope saves a daily automatic backup in its own app storage on your phone and keeps about the last week of them. Backups, CSV files, and reports you export are saved to your phone’s Downloads folder and stay there until you delete them. Other apps you allow to read your files can open them. To delete everything else, delete routes in the app, clear StopScope’s storage in Android Settings, or uninstall StopScope. That also removes the automatic backups. There is no account with us to delete. Email you send to support is kept so we can answer it and refer back to it. You can ask us to delete that email.
